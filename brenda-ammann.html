Written reflection
Rather than expecting communities to adapt to systems, place-based work creates the conditions for systems to adapt to communities.

Brenda Ammann

For me, place-based work is fundamentally about shifting power closer to the people who are most affected by decisions. It is about communities, people with lived experience, and local leaders having a greater say in what matters, what thriving looks like, and how change happens in their place.

Place-based approaches are particularly important when standardised policies, programs, and service models are not achieving the outcomes they were designed to achieve. They are most valuable when there is a gap between how people in a community experience their lives and the assumptions, decisions, and solutions being made from a distance.

Rather than expecting communities to adapt to systems, place-based work creates the conditions for systems to adapt to communities. It provides the flexibility for people in place to respond to local realities, learn from experience, and continually adapt their approaches based on what is working and what is not.

At its heart, place-based work is about building mutual understanding. It brings together people with lived experience, community members, community leaders, governments, funders, and service providers to make sense of complex challenges together. Through shared understanding, trust, and collaboration, these groups can make better decisions and take more coordinated action.
