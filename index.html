YouthWorX NT: strengthening youth employment pathways from inside community

What does it take to strengthen youth employment pathways in one of Australia's most diverse and geographically dispersed regions?

For YouthWorX NT, the answer starts with relationships. Working across the Northern Territory, the organisation combines direct support for young people with systems-level collaboration through the Greater Darwin Community Investment Committee (CIC) and the National Youth Employment Body.

In this conversation, CEO Michelle Barton shares what it means to work in community, why local leadership matters, and the lessons YouthWorX has learned about creating opportunities for young people in the Territory.

Tell us about YouthWorX NT

We're a community-embedded local grassroots organisation here in the NT. We're mainly based in the greater Darwin region, but have a presence through different activities right across the Territory.

We're very conscious that every community within the NT is different everywhere, right? And so for us, we'd never want to be a large national organisation just coming on in and going: 'we're here to save you, we can do this.' It's very much about working in community.

“Here, it's around really embedding those relationships, getting to see where the need is, where the gaps are, and where we can be invited in to contribute to that space.”

Michelle Barton, CEO, YouthWorX NT

We are a lead partner organisation for the Greater Darwin CIC and one of the members of the National Youth Employment Body since 2021. We're one of nine CICs across Australia that involves the sector, all levels of government, young people, employers and industry, and RTOs, all coming together to look at how we strengthen youth employment pathways.

The work we do at YouthWorX is probably more focused on individual outcomes and individual growth. Yes, there's still very much that systemic side to it, but it's around young people and their outcomes. Our CIC is very much about systemic change locally, but also nationally, which we get through the National Youth Employment Body.

It's also about looking at, okay, how can we collectively remove those barriers that are in place for young people, or break them down? Or actually strengthen youth employment pathways for all young people in this region, not just the individuals that we would support.

Tell us about your place, and your role within it

We're very big on employing local. We've made a really conscious decision to employ local and to employ young people where we can grow our own.

We were finding, for example, that we weren't having qualified, or just experienced, workers coming through to be able to employ. So we went, all right, let's look at it differently and let's employ some young people that want to work in the sector and let's actually put them through the education qualification. Let's skill build them. And yes, it will take longer, but that's been really important for us.

“It's really important to also just walk the talk. We're talking to employers about employing young people, employing young people with disability. We need to be leading the way in that as well.”

Michelle Barton, CEO, YouthWorX NT

“There's that additional level of, this is our community and this is who we are and where we live. And so we want to make it the best possible place for our young people and then children's children…”

Michelle Barton, CEO, YouthWorX NT

What values and principles are integral to the work your organisation does?

Involving young people in our decision making is really important. For us, it's ensuring we do it in a really meaningful way that keeps that continuous improvement happening.

It's not just bringing a young person along to the meeting and saying 'yay, we've got young people at the meeting'. That's not meaningful really, because then young people are just giving their thoughts and their feedback but not actually getting to be part of the action and decision-making.

It's hard, because at the same time, we want young people to also be in employment and on their own journey. If we're talking about youth employment pathways and some of those barriers, we want some young people who really are having some of those struggles and challenges included, and they might have some other stuff going on as well. So, I guess it's being really mindful of that and how we do that well, that has been really important.

“For us, it has also been and continues to be really important that decision-making is very much shared and equal…”

Michelle Barton, CEO, YouthWorX NT

What are the most important lessons other practitioners could take on board?

Honestly, for us, having been part of the National Youth Employment Body has been really valuable. It can be really easy to stay quite insular. You can get quite stuck in where you are, in those particular barriers, or practice frameworks or wins. So being able to come together and share those and learn from each other in that safe space is really, really valuable.

Thinking back to your core values, one of the things that's really important for us is the practice of advantage thinking, which is very much your strengths-based approach and your solution-focused approach combined. We have to remind our CIC members sometimes that we understand there's these challenges, but we're not here to focus on the deficits of government or community or young people. That doesn't get us anywhere.

“We're here to actually look at and talk about the strengths and how we can build on those and develop solutions from there.”

Michelle Barton, CEO, YouthWorX NT

So even having that common framework and common language, you can come back to that together too, right? That's really important to keep us working together. Having that small but national community of practice is really important. We can't do this alone.
